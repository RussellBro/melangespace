Nobody has measured what's under the Moon's craters.

Orbital instruments infer subsurface water at kilometre scale. No mission has measured it in place. Our probes emplace a hundred instruments below the surface in a single mission and return data from where rovers can't reach.

The probe

Released from altitude, free-falling to impact, coming to rest one to three metres below the surface. Water, temperature, and seismic sensors in a body built to survive the landing.

The network

Every impact is a timed seismic source recorded by the probes already in the ground. One deployment is both the receiver array and its own active experiment, the first distributed seismic network on the Moon since Apollo.
